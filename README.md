# Ka0s Combat Trail

![WoW](https://img.shields.io/badge/WoW-Midnight_12.1.5_and_Forever_1.60.1-purple)
![License](https://img.shields.io/badge/License-MIT-orange)
![Standard](https://img.shields.io/badge/Ka0s-WoW_Addon_Standard-yellow)
![Tests](https://img.shields.io/badge/Tests-1865%2F1865_passing-green)

Supports Retail and WoW Forever. Combat Trail shows what you and your pet just cast as a row of icons sliding along two lanes, with tick marks for the global cooldown, so you can see your rotation as you play it. Every time you fight, a small DPS window climbs with your damage, draws it as a chart and sets it against your best fight on the same boss or target. When the fight is over it is saved, with its spells, casts, gear, talents and the consumables you used, so you can look back at a pull and see what changed. On Retail, Race mode (what used to be the DPS Race addon) times a fight on a training dummy against a set length and keeps your best on each dummy and length.

All the numbers come from Blizzard's built-in damage meter, so you don't need another meter running. Everything can be set up on the addon's page under Settings → AddOns, or from chat with `/ct` (or `/combattrail`).

## Usage

The two lanes are there as soon as you log in: the Player lane, and the Pet lane docked just under it. Each cast slides in at one end and drifts across until it fades out, a red cross marks a cast that was interrupted or canceled, and big cooldowns glow. To move them, type `/ct lanes unlock` (or open Edit Mode) and drag the lanes where you want them, then `/ct lanes lock` (`/ct lanes toggle` flips it). The lanes lock apart from the HUD and the windows: `/ct lock` and `/ct unlock` never move them, and *Lock the lanes* on General → Master controls keeps Edit Mode from unlocking them. A lane can also sit on your player or pet unit frame, found by itself for ElvUI, Shadowed Unit Frames, Cell, PitBull, Unhalted Unit Frames and Blizzard's own frames, or on any frame you name. The Lanes settings page has a lane picker at the top and seven tabs under it for everything about the lane it names: size and direction, icons, background and border, ticks and the label, when it shows, and its ignored spells. Tick *Link to Player lane* on the Pet lane and it mirrors the Player lane's look, keeping its own place and label. `/ct lane pet off` turns a lane off from chat (`/ct lane player on` brings one back). Auto attacks are ignored out of the box. `/ct ignore add Hunter's Mark` hides another spell from the Player lane (add `pet` at the end for the Pet lane), and `/ct ignore remove`, `/ct ignore list` and `/ct ignore reset` manage the list (`list` writes the spells to the debug console, one a line, and says so in chat).

The DPS HUD opens as each fight starts, even if you closed it during the last one (*Open when a fight starts*, on by default; with it off, on your first hit), and fades a few seconds after the fight. It shows the fight clock, who you are fighting, your damage and DPS (your pet's damage beside it), a pace bar against your best fight on that boss or target, a damage chart with icons for your big cooldowns, and your top spells, with pet spells marked by a paw. While you fight, the game hides the real numbers from addons, so the HUD can show them and fill bars with them but can't do math on them: no live percentages or "ahead by" figure. Once the fight is over, the card shows your final damage and DPS, each spell's share, a *New best* chip when you beat your best, and whether the fight was saved. `/ct hud show` and `/ct hud hide` open and close it, `/ct hud pin` keeps it from fading, its title bar carries Results, Combat Sequence, Lock, Settings and Close buttons (and on Retail, with Race mode on, a flag that opens and closes the Race window, beside Results), and the DPS HUD settings page sets when it opens, what it paces against, how often it refreshes (*HUD refresh rate*, 10 times a second by default, which also paces the Race window) and how every part of it looks. Out of the box it is 260 wide, its least (a profile made before that keeps 390), with five spell bars and the Combat Sequence's five rows under them, and it keeps one height: nothing during or after a fight moves it, only opening or closing the Combat Sequence, the rows settings, or a drag of its corner. With no fight yet it keeps the same layout, each section where a fight puts it and waiting empty (*No target*, dashes for the figures and the pet, the damage chart's frame with *No fight yet*), so nothing jumps when a fight starts or ends; a part you switch off on the Layout tab stays off either way. Unlock it and drag the corner to change its width and how many rows fit: with the Combat Sequence open under the spells, a row at a time to each, the larger side giving first as it shrinks and the smaller side growing first, never under three spell rows and three sequence rows; with it closed or detached, the spell bars alone, at least three; and roll the mouse wheel over the spells to scroll the rest. The Combat Sequence button (a list mark), or `/ct sequence` (`/ct log` still works), opens and closes the Combat Sequence under the spell rows: the order of what you did and what happened to your buffs and debuffs, with no amounts. It lists your casts and your pet's, the buffs you gain and lose (BUFF+ and BUFF-), the debuffs put on you and the ones you put on your target, each as it lands and as it ends (DEBUFF+ and DEBUFF-), and your death, newest at the top (click the Time header to flip it), in Time, Event and Spell columns, each left-aligned under a header band like the Results list's, the spell's icon one character before its name. It never shows the damage you deal or take: the game gives addons no per-hit numbers. It clears at every pull and shows only that fight, from a few seconds before the pull on, and keeps adding lines after the fight until the next pull. It never opens by itself: closed, it stays closed through the next pull. Its filter chips (Casts, Pet, Buffs and Debuffs) are hidden until the eye beside its heading shows them under it, and hide a kind of line until the next pull. The mouse wheel scrolls back through older lines, with a thin bar at the right like Spell breakdown's, and pauses the sequence until you press *Resume*. Its pop-out button (a right chevron), or `/ct sequence detach`, moves it into a window of its own, *Combat Trail · Combat Sequence*, with an Info column that marks an interrupted cast: it stays up on its own, with or without the HUD, and you can drag and resize it anywhere. Its dock button (a left chevron), or `/ct sequence dock`, puts it back under the spell rows. The Combat Sequence settings page (its gear on the window opens it) has the rest: whether it records at all, the drawer's rows and chips, the window's rows and chips (the window's own eye shows them too), lock, scale and opacity, oldest or newest first, how fast new rows appear (instant, at most one every so many milliseconds, or in batches: it only slows what you see, never what is saved), the text size and row height, clock time instead of time from the pull, whether the Spell and Info columns show, which events show (auto attacks are hidden unless you turn them on), row tints, and what each fight saves (400 lines by default, up to 1000).

Fights are saved if they pass the recording filters on the History page: 30 seconds or longer by default (15 on WoW Forever), and only the kinds of content you keep ticked. A fight on a training dummy is saved as a Training dummies fight: the built-in dummies count on Retail, and so does any creature you add yourself, with `/ct dummy add` on your target or on the History page's Training dummies tab (on WoW Forever too, where none is built in). `/ct dummy` lists yours in the debug console, and `/ct dummy remove` takes one off or switches a built-in off. Open the Results window with `/ct results`, or with the chart button on the HUD's title bar, which opens it on the fight the card is showing. It lists your fights with filters for character, spec, content, boss or target, difficulty and date (the target named with its level, as in *Bluegill Raider (L29)*, a skull level reading *(L??)* in red), grouped by day and character unless you pick another grouping (each fight's gap to the best is still measured against its own boss and difficulty). On Retail a Fights / Races / All filter (or `/ct results races`) lists your races on their own, with filters for the race length, the dummy kind, Hide flagged, Include incomplete and Only best, and groupings by race length and dummy kind; a race shows its race DPS, a chip with its length and one with its flag, and its detail adds the race's Timing and Flags, where a right-click ignores a late cast. The *Insights* tab (or `/ct results insights`) adds up every saved fight the filters pass: stat cards, your DPS progression as a share of each board's best, each board with its specs side by side and how consistent they are, your fights by character and by content, and your recent new bests; on Retail, under the Races filter, it adds the race sections: races by length, the late casts, races per day and the damage after the buzzer. Each row shows how far the fight sits from the best, a trophy on the best itself, the trinkets and consumables it used (with the world, camp, zone and event buffs you pulled with, such as Camp Benefits on WoW Forever; on Retail only the older world, Darkmoon Faire and holiday buffs are known so far, so most fights there list none; plain food and drink are left out, and buff food shows as Well Fed, whatever its buff is called) and the start of your note, and you can drag the Character column wider. The window closes when you enter combat, unless you turn that off on the History page. On WoW Forever, where there are no specializations, the talent tree you spent the most points in stands in for the spec everywhere. Click a fight for its detail: damage, DPS, how far it sits from the best in its group, its spells, gear, talents and what it used, each section folding under its own arrow. The detail is one scrolling page with no tabs, and its *Combat Sequence* section, under Targets and open until you fold it, shows the fight's saved Combat Sequence, with chips that count and filter casts, pet casts, buffs and debuffs, and a scroll bar; click a cast there to open the timeline on it. A fight saved before the Combat Sequence came in has none, and one saved while it was still the Combat Log shows only its casts, buffs, debuffs and deaths: its old hit, heal and target-buff lines are left out. From there, the *Timeline* button at the bottom shows when you cast each ability next to another fight, as its icon or a tick, yours and your pet's in their own colors, *Compare* sets two fights side by side spell by spell, with the trinkets and consumables each used, and *View gear* lays out the gear and stats you pulled with. Right-click a fight to add a note, share it to chat, compare with it or delete it. The Results window, the timeline, Compare and the gear view each have a gear button beside the close button that opens the settings. Fights are kept for 7 days, except a fight that holds a best, and `/ct purge` clears old ones right away. The damage chart of a fight is only there until you reload or log out, because the game won't let addons save the in-combat numbers.

Race mode is what used to be the DPS Race addon, now part of Combat Trail on Retail. A race is a fight on a training dummy timed to a set length: your first hit starts the clock, a buzzer ends it, and the damage you did in that time is your score. Each race is saved in your history like any other fight, and your best is kept per character, spec, kind of dummy and race length.

Walk up to a training dummy and the Race window and the DPS HUD open side by side by themselves, as one panel: the Race window sits flush against the HUD and, on its left or right, takes the HUD's height (or type `/ct race show`, or click the flag on the HUD's title bar). Pick a length (0:30, 1:00, 2:00, 3:00, 5:00, or *Custom* up to 15:00), check your best for that dummy and length and your top three races on it, and press *Arm Race*, or type `/ct race arm` (`/ct race arm 90`, `1:30` or `3m`). While the race runs, the Race window counts down the time left over a bar drawn like the HUD's pace bar that empties to the buzzer, both turning from green to yellow to red as time runs out, and shows the best you are after (its damage and DPS side by side), where that best stood at this second with your current damage beside it, and its damage at each quarter of the race with yours as you passed it, each with a thin pace bar that fills green or red like the HUD's. The DPS HUD beside it shows your live damage, chart and spells and the full pace bar against the same best; both clocks move together, and the countdown, the HUD's clock and the center numbers always agree. The last ten seconds count down in big numbers in the middle of the screen, changing in the same frame as the Race window's countdown, and a voice says the last five; at the buzzer it shows and says STOP. A race that ends early (you leave combat or die before the buzzer) shows and says FAILED, and a new best says NEW BEST at its result, each in the voice you pick on the Race mode page: eight voices ship, an arena announcer by default. You pick when the numbers start, or turn them or the STOP off, on the Race mode page. Then the window shows the damage still landing after the buzzer, how long you stay in combat and all four quarters, with *Stop Now* to take the result without waiting out that damage, and a hint for ways to drop combat if it holds. The result card shows your score, your race DPS, how it compares to your best and any flags: a cast started late after the buzzer, damage that started again after a pause, a `/reload` after the buzzer, or dying. Under them, *Against your best* sets this race's score, DPS, item level and date beside your best's, with the difference, and *Top races* lists your top three races there, with this one highlighted and ranked. From there, *New Race* goes back to the start, *Open* shows the race in the Results window, and *Share* posts it to chat. A new best also shows a toast near the top of the screen; a fight's new best can toast too, off until you turn on *Toast a fight's new best* under History → New-best toast.

`/ct race cancel` takes a race off at any point; the fight goes on as a plain dummy fight. If you leave combat before the buzzer the race is saved as incomplete and never counts as a best. A `/reload` mid-race carries the race on where it was. The Race mode settings page holds the switch at its top, the default length, auto-show near a training dummy (and whether the DPS HUD opens with it) and auto-arm (off by default), the race clock, the countdown (from 3 to 20 seconds) and the buzzer, the scoring rules, the center call-out's own look and voice on one Callout tab (a live preview, a test countdown and a drag to move it on top, then Look and Sound side tabs, with a glow behind the text you can tune as in AuraMaster), and the late-cast ignore list: spells whose casts after the buzzer never flag a race, which `/ct race ignore` shows (in the debug console) and edits too. It also sets whether the Race window stays attached to the DPS HUD, on which side, and whether it matches the HUD's height. Unlock the frames and drag the Race window's corner to resize it: beside the HUD at its height it changes only the width. In the Results window, the Fights / Races / All filter (or `/ct results races`) lists your races with their own filters and groupings, and the Insights tab adds race sections under Races. `/ct test race` holds a sample race in the Race window, and `/ct sim race-newbest` (or `race-behind`, `race-first`, `race-incomplete`, `race-died`, `race-flagged`, `race-shared`, `race-sticky`) plays one through. Race mode is not on WoW Forever. The Race window has no minimap entry, but on Retail you can bind a key to *Toggle Race window* (below), or put `/ct race toggle` or `/ct race arm` in a macro.

To set things up without a fight, turn on test mode with `/ct test`: the lanes loop sample casts, the HUD holds still on a made-up fight with a sample Combat Sequence (made-up casts, buffs and debuffs, never saved) and the Results window opens on a made-up history whose fights carry one too, each with a strip saying the numbers are samples. `/ct sim` plays a whole made-up fight in real time instead, from the first hit to the card and then into the Results window, and `/ct sim newbest`, `behind`, `first`, `short` or `lost` picks the ending. Both draw the same sample set on every character. A new best in the sim brings its toast (and on Retail, with Race mode on, the center call-out's NEW BEST), as `/ct test race result` does; both stop when you enter combat, their toasts and call-outs go when they stop, and nothing from either is saved. The addon's look follows a skin. `/ct skin` opens the skin picker, and while it is up the addon shows in test mode (the eye beside the list turns that off and on) so you can see each skin on the lanes and the HUD. Press *Apply* to keep the skin you picked, or close the picker to put everything back. The minimap button opens the settings on a left-click, and a right-click menu turns the addon on or off, locks the HUD and the windows, toggles test mode or the sim and shows the HUD. You can bind keys to toggle the HUD, the Results window and the lanes, and on Retail the Race window (*Toggle Race window*), in Options → Keybindings → Ka0s Combat Trail, or on General → Keybindings in this addon's settings. Closing the HUD with its key, the minimap menu or `/ct hud toggle` closes the Race window with it; opening it opens the HUD alone, except near a training dummy, where the Race window opens with it.

From chat, `/ct enable` and `/ct disable` turn the addon on and off without reloading, `/ct config` opens its settings, `/ct list` shows every setting and `/ct get`, `/ct set` and `/ct reset` read, change or reset one by name (`/ct set history.days 14`). `/ct resetall` puts every setting back after asking first. `/ct version` prints the version, `/ct perf` measures what the addon costs you during a fight, and `/ct probe` writes a report of what your client tells addons about the meter, your casts and your pet, which helps when something looks off on one client. `/ct debug` and `/ct diagnostics` are for bug reports (see below).

Everything else is on the addon's page under Settings → AddOns, and `/ct help` (or `/combattrail help`) prints the full command list.

## How the trail and the HUD work

- Each lane hears the game's own cast events for you or your pet. A finished cast adds its icon at the "now" edge, and every icon slides away at a steady speed, so its distance from the edge is how long ago you cast it.
- A fight starts when you enter combat and follows Blizzard's damage meter: one meter session is one fight. Feign Death, Vanish and Shadowmeld don't split a fight if you are back in combat within a few seconds, and a boss whose meter split in two is joined back into one fight.
- During the fight the HUD copies the meter's numbers straight into its bars and text, so it always shows what the meter shows.
- A couple of seconds after you leave combat and your damage stops, the addon reads the fight's totals from the meter. Your DPS is your damage plus your pet's, divided by the length of the fight.
- The fight is saved if it passes the recording filters. Your best on a boss or target is the saved fight with the highest DPS for that character and spec, and it is worked out from the saved fights each time.

## FAQ

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Why doesn't the HUD show percentages or how far ahead I am while I fight? | In combat the game hides the meter's numbers from addons. An addon can put them in a bar or print them, but it can't divide, add or compare them. The fight-over card has the percentages and the difference once combat ends. |
| Why can't I see the damage chart of an older fight? | The chart is built from the meter's in-combat numbers, which the game won't let addons save. A fight keeps its chart until you reload or log out. Everything else about it is saved. |
| Why wasn't my fight saved? | It was shorter than the minimum length, its content is unticked on the History page's recording filters, or the meter lost it (a reset or a logout before it was read). The fight-over card says which. |
| Why is my DPS a little different from Blizzard's meter? | Combat Trail adds your pet's damage to yours and divides by the fight's length as the meter reports it. The meter's own DPS figure is saved with the fight too, in the *Meter DPS* box. |
| Does it read the combat log? | No. Everything comes from the built-in damage meter and the game's cast and aura events. That is why the Combat Sequence shows the order of your casts, buffs and debuffs but no damage per hit. |
| Does it work on WoW Forever? | Yes, from the same download. Delves, arenas and Mythic+ don't exist there, so their settings and filters don't appear. |
| Why does the Combat Sequence show no buffs or debuffs during a fight on WoW Forever? | WoW Forever hides buffs and debuffs from addons during combat, so the Buffs and Debuffs rows show only the changes read as combat ends, such as the buffs you lose when you die. Your casts and your pet's still show throughout. The Buffs and Debuffs chips and their settings say so on WoW Forever. |
| What happened to DPS Race? | It is Race mode now, on Retail, inside Combat Trail. A race is a fight on a training dummy timed to a set length, saved in the same history as your other fights. |
| Why did my race end early when combat dropped for a moment? | A race ends the first time you leave combat before the buzzer, even when Feign Death or Vanish keeps the fight itself going. The race is then incomplete, and an incomplete race never counts as a best. |
| Why is my race flagged? | A cast started after the buzzer's grace, your damage started again after a pause, you reloaded after the buzzer, or you died. The Flags section of its detail says which. A spell on the late-cast ignore list never flags a race. |
| Why does my race score more than it did in DPS Race? | Combat Trail adds your pet's damage to your own, in a race as in any fight. |
| Are my fights shared with anyone? | No. They stay in your own saved variables unless you share one to chat yourself. |

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|
| The HUD stays empty in a fight | Make sure Blizzard's damage meter is on. The HUD reads its numbers from it. |
| A lane doesn't show | Check `/ct lane player on`, then the lane's Visibility tab: it may be set to show only in combat, or hidden while mounted or resting. |
| A lane anchored to a unit frame moved to the middle of the screen | That frame wasn't found, so the lane went back to a free position. Pick another frame on the Size and position tab, or name the frame yourself. |
| A spell I don't care about fills the lane | `/ct ignore add` with the spell's name, or add it on the lane's Ignored spells tab. |
| I can't find the HUD or the Results window | `/ct hud show` or `/ct results`. Reset position on General → Master controls puts every window back in its starting place. |
| The settings panel won't open | It doesn't open in combat. Try again once combat ends. |
| Something looks wrong and I want to report it | Follow [Reporting a bug](#reporting-a-bug) below. |

## Reporting a bug

- Type `/ct debug on` and reproduce the bug.
- Type `/ct diagnostics`.
- If the debug window isn't open, open it with `/ct debug`. Press **Copy**, copy the entire output, and include it with your bug report.

The report is added after the debug trace in the same window, so one copy carries both.
